Week 8: Teaching Poetic Traditions

March 2, 2022

In class

  • Candace Myers' teaching demo:
    • The Passionate Shepherd To His Love
    • The Nymph's Reply To The Shepherd
  • Poems and poetic traditions:
    1. Elizabethan Poetry
      • William Shakespeare Sonnet 130
    2. Restoration Poetry
      • Alexander Pope's "An Essay on Criticism," Part 1
    3. Romanticism
      • Walt Whitman "When I Heard the Learn'd Astronomer"
    4. Victorian Poetry
      • Elizabeth Barrett Browning, "[How do I love thee? Let me count the ways]"
    5. Imagism
      • H.D. "Oread"
    6. Modernism
      • T.S. Eliot "The Wasteland," Part 1
    7. Black Arts Movement
      • The Last Poets, "When the Revolution Comes" (lyrics)
    8. Black Mountain Poets
      • Robert Creeley "I Know a Man"
    9. Confessional Poetry
      • Sylvia Plath "Morning Song"
    10. Language Poetry
      • Charles Bernstein, "Of Time and the Line"
    11. Concrete Poetry
      • Mary Ellen Solt, "Elegy for Three Astronauts"
    12. Performance Poetry
      • Ursula Rucker, "What a Woman Must Do"

Assignments for next class (Choosing Course Materials)

  • Readings:
    • Kolbas, E. Dean. Critical theory and the literary canon. Routledge, 2001.
  • Explore the following catalogs and resources:
    • W.W. Norton English
    • The Heath Anthology of American Literature
    • Penguin Anthologies
    • 18 Inclusive Anthologies That Highlight Underrepresented Voices
    • Online Archives like: The Emily Dickinson Archive, The William Blake Archive, and The Walt Whitman Archive
    • The WORLD WIDE WEB and beyond
  • Identify anthologies and resources that you could use for teaching the following types of courses:
    • Introduction to Literature (including specific modes: poetry, drama, fiction, etc.)
    • British Literature courses
    • American Literature courses
    • Ethnic American Literature courses (multiethnic or focused)
    • World Literature courses
    • Special Topic Literature courses
  • Prepare a brief (10-15 minute) group presentation on resources to teach your chosen type of course, discussing the advantages and limitations for each resource. Upload your presentation to this shared folder.
