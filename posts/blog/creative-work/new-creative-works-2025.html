New Creative Works: 2025

February 4, 2026

It's been a wonderfully productive year, and despite the political context we live in, I published a record number of creative works! Here's a portfolio of my creative digital works published in 2025, along with three earlier pieces republished that year.

Consequences

This work critiques Trump's first 100 Presidential Actions by extracting linguistic data (nouns, verbs, adjectives) from official White House titles to generate speculative actions. Published in Taper #14: Sonnets.

Anti-Woke Lullaby

A generative digital poem that repurposes the "Hush, Little Baby" structure to critique political complacency. Published in Michigan Quarterly Review, Issue 64:4, Fall 2025.

Punch Flag Fire

This piece critiques Trump administration measures causing immigrant and citizen loss, examining workforce and brain drain. Published in Michigan Quarterly Review, Issue 64:4, Fall 2025.

No Kings Haiku

Uses protest slogans as variables in a minimalist Python script generating random haiku. Includes functional code readers can execute using a Python compiler. Published in Run Run Run.

Tiny Protests: No Kings

An emoji-animated narrative commemorating the No Kings protests (June 14 and October 18, 2025). This five-movement piece lasts approximately 3 minutes per cycle. Published in RE-MEDIATE #4, Fall 2025.

Encounters

Generates endless emoji-driven narratives about interpersonal impact at crossroads. Published in Taper #15: Crossroads.

Juracán Borinqueño (Originally 2018)

Generates speculative hurricane scenarios in Puerto Rico, written in Spanish. Originally a Twitter bot, ported to HTML in 2023. Published in Antología Lit(e)Lat, Volumen 2.

Protestitas 2023 (Originally earlier)

Generates an endless protest march with Puerto Rican demonstrators chanting real protest slogans. A rescue and update of the original Twitter bot. Published in Antología Lit(e)Lat, Volumen 2.

It's Complicated (Originally 2023)

Generates hypothetical Puerto Ricans expressing opinions on statehood, commonwealth, or independence. Originally published in The Los Angeles Review. Republished in Antología Lit(e)Lat, Volumen 2.

Technical Notes

These works are examples of "Cyborg Digital Writing," with most pieces vibe-coded using ChatGPT (except "No Kings Haiku," which was hand-coded). For more on this approach, see my Cyborg Digital Writing Primer.
